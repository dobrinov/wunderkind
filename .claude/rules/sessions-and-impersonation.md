# Controllers, child profiles, impersonation, changelog

Controllers: `AuthenticatedController` is the base for logged-in pages (session auth, `current_user` from `session[:user_id]`); auth endpoints are rate-limited. The session carries two identities: `signed_in_user` is whoever typed the password and `session[:child_id]` is the managed child whose profile is open, so `current_user` — what every other line of the app reads — is the child while a profile is open, and the practice, rating, XP and streak all land on the child. `ChildSessionsController` (`POST /switch-child/:id`, `DELETE /switch-child`) is the only way in and out — and the way *out* of a child's profile (to a sibling's or back to the parent) asks for the parent's PIN when one is set (`users.profile_pin_digest`, `User#profile_pin`, set in Настройки under „Общ таблет" through `ProfilePinsController`; never on the way in, never while impersonating, rate-limited); it re-checks `signed_in_user.switchable_children` (managed children this login is a linked parent of) on *every* request rather than trusting the switch, `reset_session` on sign-in and sign-out drops any open profile, and while one is open the parent's own area is closed (`current_user` is a student) with `shared/_child_session_bar` in the `application` layout the only seam and the way back — it also lists the login's other managed profiles (`other_child_profiles`), because a household passes one device around and switching child should be one tap rather than a trip through the parent's own screens. An admin can also look at the app as somebody else: `/overseer/users` carries a „Прегледай" button per row and `ImpersonationsController` (`POST /impersonate/:id`, `DELETE /impersonate`, deliberately not under `/overseer` because *stopping* is pressed from inside a student's session where the admin area is shut) swaps `session[:user_id]` for the target and parks the admin's own id in `session[:impersonator_id]` — so every line of the app reads the viewed user as the signed-in one without a single conditional, and the two session mechanisms compose: an admin viewing a parent steps into that parent's managed children exactly as the parent would. Admins are not viewable, not for privilege reasons (admin is already the top role, so nothing escalates) but because the admin area is the one place a second impersonation could be started and a stack two deep has no obvious way back. It is **read-only**: `ApplicationController` refuses every non-GET request while a session is impersonated, because a graded answer given from this seat would move a real child's rating, spend their attempt and burn the question out of their assignment, and nothing afterwards could tell it apart from something the child did — this app measures children and must not measure the adult looking over their shoulder. The three exceptions write to the session and never to the database: signing out, switching child profile, and the stop button. The rule is on the verb, which cannot see the duel screens — a GET there serves a problem (stamping the player's clock, which their speed bonus is scored out of) and settles a match that ran out of clock (paying the bonuses and writing the result, since there is no job and a duel can expire with neither player making a request). Both are guarded at the write in `ChallengesController`. If the admin behind a session stops being one mid-session it is `reset_session`'d outright rather than left borrowed with nobody to hand it back to, and `shared/_analytics` measures `impersonator || current_user`, so the owner's heaviest clicking about still never reaches the Plausible dashboard. What it looks like is `shared/_impersonation_frame`, rendered as a direct child of `<body>` in all five layouts: one fixed red box over the viewport — fixed, so a long page cannot scroll the warning out of sight — with a bar naming the account, its role and the way out, and `body:has(> .impersonation-frame)` padding the page clear of it. Those rules are the one thing in the stylesheet that sits **outside** `@layer components` on purpose: a frame that any page could shift with a utility, or push its own content under, is a frame nobody can trust to be telling the truth about whose account this is. `Overseer::` is the admin area (`Overseer::BaseController` requires `current_user.admin?`, `admin` layout — the app's own header at a wider measure, `max-w-[96rem]`, for the browsers and queues that want three columns). Its people list (`Overseer::UsersController`) is by **household**: `Families` walks `parent_links` both ways in a recursive CTE, so two parents of one child are one family and families (not rows) paginate and filter on any member; a parent with nobody linked is a family of one, and students nobody is linked to and the old flat list are tabs of their own. A person's page is a student's (`StudentRecord` for the numbers and recent sessions) or, for a parent or an admin with children, a grown-up's (`ChildOverview` per child, `FirstSteps` read off existing columns — nothing is recorded for it — and only the email facts that are stored; there is no sent-mail log). Its actions mail a reset link or a confirmation (only while `Mailing.enabled?`), unlink a child (refused when the parent's login is a managed child's only door; a managed child with an email is released instead), and delete an account through `AccountDeletion`, which handles the foreign keys no `dependent:` covers — leagues pass on, open invitations are withdrawn, a live duel is abandoned, a suggestion loses its credit — in one transaction, and takes with it the managed children no other parent holds (the rest pass to another parent — see co-parents). Layouts: `application` (sticky nav, plus a `shared/_tabbar` bottom nav for students at phone widths), `modal` (answer flow), `landingpage` (the public marketing shell: header with Вход + Създай профил and section anchors, no app nav and no reward pills, plus the footer), `simple` (auth), all sharing `shared/_head`. The `modal` layout has two shapes: a page that sets `content_for :bare` owns all of its own chrome and gets a full-bleed gray page (the practice flow and duels, which supply `shared/_practice_header` or `challenges/_scoreboard`); anything else keeps the original white dialog with the back/close row. `back_path`/`close_path` params must pass through the `internal_path` helper (XSS guard).

What changed since you were last here: `config/changelog.yml` is the whole of it, read by `Changelog` and written by a person, with a version, a date and the entries in both locales. `users.last_changelog_version` holds the newest release that user has been *shown*; everything above it is unseen, and the `shared/_changelog_dialog` in the `application` layout lists it. A `<dialog>` and not a styled div, because only `showModal()` gives the top layer, the focus trap and an inert page, and this is a dialog in the strict sense; Its list is `flex-auto`, not `flex-1`: the dialog has a max-height and no height, and real Mobile Safari gave a basis-0 child of such a box nothing — on an iPhone the notes were 32px tall, unreadable and unscrollable, while Chrome and Playwright's WebKit both drew it fine, so only a check in the iOS Simulator shows it. On a phone it takes nearly the whole screen with 16px notes, and the page under any modal dialog (`html:has(dialog[open])`) is held still so the finger's scroll reaches the list. Esc and a backdrop click press the same button rather than closing silently, since a release note that can be dismissed without answering is one people learn to dismiss without reading. The button is a form, because the answer can only be remembered on the server — `PATCH /changelog` stamps the newest version there is, and `/changelog` is the page for going back and reading all of it, which stamps too (reading it *is* being told). Versions are compared with `Gem::Version` rather than counted, so "1.10.0" is newer than "1.9.0" and a column holding a version since deleted from the file still resolves; a column holding something that is not a version at all says nothing rather than everything. A new account is stamped current on create (`User` `before_create`), so a child signing up today is not greeted by a wall of releases they were never around for — NULL means the opposite, *nobody has ever told this person anything*, which is why every account predating the file gets the backlog once. The dialog is keyed to the *login* (`signed_in_user`), not to the profile open on it, and answering it stamps the login and every managed child (`ApplicationController#acknowledge_changelog!`): a parent who dismissed the notes and then switched into a child's profile used to be shown them again as the child. Nothing is shown or stamped while an admin is impersonating: the notes are addressed to that person, and the only button is a write that session refuses. `/discord` (`CommunityController`) is our own address for the invite: the static error pages in `public/` (written by hand, Bulgarian, no assets) link there, a signed-in student is shown a „for grown-ups“ page instead, and with no invite set it goes home. `shared/_discord_note` is the one-line invitation where the app falls short — the calendar's „what we do not prepare for“, a competition marked on written solutions (`mixed`/`proof`), a parent's thin focus topics — offered to grown-ups and, on public pages, to visitors (`Community.offered_on_public_page?`). Each new release is also posted to the Discord server's #новини (`ChangelogAnnouncement`, `rake changelog:announce`, run by the host's `deploy.sh` after the healthcheck, through `DISCORD_NEWS_WEBHOOK_URL`; the last version announced is a file on the storage volume, and a first run only records it). The Discord invite itself is `DISCORD_INVITE_URL` (`Community`), linked for grown-ups only — in Настройки and the weekly report — since Discord is 13+. The file's own rules are in its header and guarded by `spec/requests/changelog_spec.rb` — ascending order, both locales filled, and no release note that mentions a controller, a migration or an Elo, because the audience is a ten-year-old and their parent.
