# Adaptivity: Dispatcher, grade hold, placement

**There are no school grades anywhere in the model** — a first-grader who can do third-grade work gets it, and a fifth-grader who can't doesn't. `users.grade` and `questions.grade_min`/`grade_max` were dropped; difficulty is the question's Elo and ability is the student's, full stop. The one exception is optional and read by nothing that picks a problem: a student may carry `users.birth_year` (set in Настройки or when a parent creates a child; a grown-up is never asked) and `users.grade_offset` (−2..2, a start a year or two early or late, a repeated year counting as late), from which `SchoolGrade` estimates the grade — first grade in the school year of the calendar year the child turns seven, up a grade every 15 September, `:preschool`/`:finished` outside 1–12. `User#estimated_grade` is shown as „≈ 3 клас“ on the parent's children list and child page and in the admin user list. One thing is deliberately built on it, from production data: a quarter of all answers were „не съм го учил", concentrated in topics school had not reached (fractions and decimals skipped three times in five by first- and third-graders), and Elo cannot tell a hard problem from an untaught one. So `Dispatcher.held_topic_ids` holds back topics the МОН programme (`Curriculum::TOPICS`) first lists more than `GRADE_REACH` (one) grade ahead of the estimate, from everything the app picks on its own — filler, stretch, the frontier, placement — unless the child has answered one of them correctly, and never a topic asked for by name (`available_pool(user, allow:)`: a parent's focus, a competition, a review pick). No birth year, no hold. The hold also works *inside* the topics it lets through, per problem: a topic is broad („Числа и редици" holds tens and units and negative temperatures, „Ъгли" the kinds of angle and the triangle's angle sum), and a second-grader in the UX review was served both from topics school had reached. `questions.taught_from_grade` is the grade that first teaches a problem (`TaughtFrom`): a past paper's own grade, a ladder family's grade from `config/problem_type_grades.yml` (a person's reading of the МОН programme — one grade, or one per rung for families whose numbers grow out of a grade, read off the rung in the problem file because a question's Elo drifts; an unlisted family takes its topic's first grade), or, for an arithmetic fact, its own numbers; the hand-written batches have none and are held by topic only. `Dispatcher.within_grade` keeps out anything above `taught_ceiling` — the estimated grade plus `GRADE_REACH`, or the last grade whose `Curriculum` Elo window starts at or below the child's rating, whichever is later, so a strong child is never capped — everywhere the app picks, a focus and the frontier included; only a competition the child entered and a problem type opened by name pass `beyond_grade: true`. The importer writes the column; `rake questions:taught_from` rewrites it for the whole bank after the map changes. Since the hold and placement both read the birth year, a parent edits it (with the school start, and a managed child's name) from the child's page at `/parents/children/:id/profile/edit` (`Parents::ChildProfilesController`), and the page asks for it while it is missing. Placement reads the grade too (`Placement.grade_bounds`): it opens at the start of the grade below and stops doubling its step at the end of the grade above, and a skip in placement parks the topic like a skip anywhere else. Placement is the hero of „Днес" while `Placement.due?`, in place of the session button: in production most new students who left placement never answered a problem.

Adaptivity (no AI/LLM integration — it was built and then removed by decision; question content is generated outside the app): `Dispatcher` (app/services/dispatcher.rb) is the single answer to "which problems suit this student right now" — it owns the pools (`practice_pool`, and `available_pool`, which drops topics the student has deferred by skipping), the difficulty target (`TARGET_SUCCESS_RATE` inverted through the Elo expectation, so the aim sits ~147 points below the student), the cold start (`starting_rating` = the Elo of the easiest problem in the practice pool, applied by `User.new_student`; starting low is the cheap mistake, and the floor is the one point on the distribution that does not move as the bank grows — a percentile did), and calibration (`calibrating?` until `CALIBRATION_ANSWERS` *measured* answers and only while `users.placed_at` is nil; `calibration_rungs` / `at_rung` build a ladder climbing `CALIBRATION_CLIMB` above the student). A new student is placed *visibly* before any of that: `Placement` (app/services/placement.rb) runs an eight-question session of its own at sign-up and ends on a screen that names the student's band. It is an ordinary `Assignment` of `kind: :placement`, so the practice screen, the answer form, the sounds and the report control all work unchanged — the only thing it adds is choosing each next question from the answers so far. **Gallop, then bisect**: a pure binary search would open in the middle of the bank, which for most children is a problem they cannot read, so the first question sits near the floor and the step *doubles* (`CLIMB`) while answers are right; the first wrong answer closes a bracket and the rest of the session bisects it. That places a student within a few dozen Elo points across the whole 600-2800 range, and nobody is asked to start in the middle. A skip counts with the misses, because „не съм го учил“ is the honest answer to a topic school has not reached and reading it as „too hard for now“ beats making the child guess. The session is **unmeasured** (`Assignment::UNMEASURED_KINDS`, which `UserAnswer.measured` reads too, so the list cannot drift): its whole job is to find a rating, and letting the per-answer Elo run as well would move that rating twice by two different methods — but XP, the streak and badges are paid, because it is still eight problems a child sat down and did. `Placement.due?` is the one gate (`PlacementsController`, the home-page card and sign-up all ask it) and it is deliberately *not* „has no `placed_at`“: every account predating the feature has none, and replacing a rating hundreds of real answers converged on with an eight-question estimate would be a downgrade dressed as a feature — so it asks `Dispatcher.calibrating?`, which is the question „is this rating still a guess?“ and which `placed_at` already closes. `Assignment#complete?` exists for this kind: a placement is built one question at a time, so „nothing left unanswered“ is its state after *every* answer and cannot mean „finished“. Everything that reads the session reads `Placement.rows`, never the cached association — by the time `advance!` runs, the answer that triggered it was saved through a different instance of the same row, and a search that cannot see the last answer re-asks it and places everybody at the floor. Sign-up lands on `GET /placements/new`, a welcome screen that says what is about to happen (a redirect is a GET, and `create` is not); an abandoned session resumes rather than starting a second one (`Placement.unfinished`). The old invisible ladder is still underneath and still works, for anyone who skips the session. `SessionComposer` owns the *mix*, not the difficulty: a calibrating student gets `calibration_questions` (the ladder rotated across unlocked topics, so the curriculum graph still holds), everyone else gets spaced review due topics + frontier topics unlocked by `topic_prerequisites` + one stretch question + filler at the dispatcher's target. Two variety guards run there: `MAX_PER_TOPIC` spreads the frontier across topics, and `MAX_PER_SHAPE` (with the `diversify` pass, which recognises a template via `ProblemSeeds.shape_of` — numbers blanked, and names, a capitalised word that does not start the text, and the picture patterns' shape words (`SHAPE_WORDS`) too — mirrored step for step in SQL as `SHAPE_SQL`, which a spec holds equal) caps problems sharing one template — without it the arithmetic fact tables, being thousands of `a + b` rows in one topic, fill every young session; the cap yields rather than let a session shrink. Review and frontier picks reach no further than `SessionComposer::REACH` (the duel's 300 points) from the student's target (`Dispatcher.nearest(..., reach:)`): a topic with nothing that close contributes nothing and the right-difficulty filler takes its place, where it used to hand over its closest problems however far they were. `AnswerSubmission` schedules spaced review (SM-2-ish intervals on `skills`), detects topic mastery (rating ≥ 1400, ≥ 10 games → `mastered_at`, XP bonus), and halves XP for hinted correct answers. Hint ladders live in `question_hints` (a `ladder` of strings). The ladder never reaches the page: each click fetches one rung from `HintRevealsController` (`POST /questions/:question_id/hint`), which counts the reveal into `assignment_questions.hints_revealed` — the server-side number that halves a hinted correct answer's XP, taken from the row and never from the request, the same stance the duel clock takes on its speed bonus. Two ways in: typed in the admin question editor's „Подсказки" section, where `reviewed_at` gates them because a human is mid-edit, or shipped in a problem file's `hints:` key, which arrives reviewed — the answers and explanations beside them go live on import too, and 22.6k ladders were never going to be typed in one at a time. Families emit them with `c.q(hints: [...])`; `check.rb` refuses a rung that contains the answer, and 56 families (the puzzle, kangaroo and deduction ladders — 3.2k problems) carry one so far. **A hint is not a shortened explanation**: an explanation is read after a wrong answer and states the answer, a hint is read instead of giving up and must not. Availability is the *session's* choice, not the question's: `Assignment#hints_allowed?` reads the nullable `assignments.hints_allowed` if set, else `Assignment::HINTS_BY_KIND` (practice and daily yes). Duels never reach it — they have no assignment, and a hint would be worth points to whoever used it fastest. Free-text answers always record `verdict: pending_review` (`UserAnswer#pending_review?`, which the student-facing views treat as a fourth outcome beside right/wrong/skipped rather than as wrong); nothing serves free text today — the practice pool excludes it and the homework flow that carried it was removed — so the type is reserved for the Phase 3 AI grading. The global weekly leaderboard (`/leaderboard`) is nickname-only, Sofia-time Monday reset, and lists only students with points this week — the count the home card gives; a student with none is told so rather than shown on „0“.
