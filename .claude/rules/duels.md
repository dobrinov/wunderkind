# Duels

Friends, presence, leagues and nicknames are in `friends-and-leagues.md`.

Challenges (live duels): `/challenges` pairs two students head-to-head on the same problems with one shared clock, scored on speed as well as correctness. `ChallengeMatchmaker` *is* the queue — an unmatched player is a `waiting` `Challenge` with one `challenge_participants` row, and the next player to press the button joins the closest lobby (within `MAX_GAP` rating points, or anyone who has waited past `PATIENCE`) under a row lock. Joining *fills* the lobby (`status: :lobby`) rather than starting the match: `ChallengeLobby` then runs the ready room, where both players press „Готов съм" and the last press stamps `starts_at` for a `COUNTDOWN_SECONDS` countdown, after which the next read flips it to `active` with `started_at = starts_at` — the countdown's own deadline, so the match clock is the same length for both players however late the poll that happened to start it was. There is no host and no start button, deliberately: the queue pairs strangers, and the one holding the only start button could sit on it. (A host button is the right shape for a room you invited a friend into; there is no such room yet.) The match used to begin the instant a second player arrived, which gave whoever had been waiting no moment to put their hands on the keyboard while the speed bonus was already running. Rooms nobody turns up to expire lazily on the way in — a lobby nobody joined inside `Challenge::LOBBY_TTL`, and a filled one nobody readied in inside `READY_TIMEOUT`. **Matchmaking runs on every poll of a waiting screen, not only on the button**, and `call` reaches past the player's own empty lobby to do it: treating that lobby as "a match you are already in" is what used to leave two people who pressed a second apart — or whose ratings were further than `MAX_GAP` apart on the first try — each sitting in a room of their own forever, with `PATIENCE` unable to rescue them because nothing ever looked again. Concurrency is the hard part of that, because a lock on one lobby cannot see a lock on another. Two guards: `candidates` lets a player who already holds a lobby join only an *older* one, so in any pair exactly one side qualifies and two waiting players cannot join each other; and `pair` locks **both players' user rows** in id order before seating anyone. The second is what makes "at most one match per player" hold at all — pairing is the one operation that commits somebody who is not the one asking (the host), so with three waiting lobbies A/B/C a player can be taking a seat in one while a third player takes a seat in theirs, and end up in two matches at once with somebody's opponent elsewhere. Every pairing either player is part of contends for the same row lock, which is where that is caught; ordering by id keeps it deadlock-free. A room both players readied in and then walked away from is written off by `ChallengeLobby.begin!` rather than started, since a match that is over before its first paint would otherwise pay both of them the draw bonus for a duel neither was at. A poll that moves a player into somebody else's room answers with a `redirect`, since the page they are on is no longer their match. A duel can also be about something: `/challenges` carries a **lobby browser** — who is waiting (and whether they are near your level — never their band), the room's configuration and what it is about — because the queue used to be a button that either found somebody or did not, and a child who wants to play a particular person or a particular topic had no way to say so. Rooms are listed by nickname only, like the scoreboard and the leaderboard, and refresh themselves in a `turbo-frame` (`poll_controller`) rather than reloading the page under somebody reading it — the join button carries `turbo_frame: "_top"`, since a match rendered inside a list of lobbies is not a match. Joining off the list has **no rating window**: MAX_GAP governs what a student is matched into *without being asked*, and a child who looked at a stronger player and chose anyway is allowed to. `challenge_topics` holds the categories a room is restricted to, empty meaning anything — which is what every duel was before this and still what most are, so the fast path is one press. A category is one of the thirteen **root** topics and not one of the forty-two leaves, because the list is read by a nine-year-old and „Събиране на дроби с еднакви знаменатели“ is a syllabus entry rather than a choice. `DuelCategories.for(user)` is the offer, and the reason it is a service rather than `Topic.roots` is that **the bank is not square**: Тригонометрия has nothing below 1800 and Аритметика nothing above 2200, so offering every category to everybody hands a nine-year-old five problems a thousand points over their head — `Dispatcher` widens until it finds something, so this fails as a bad match rather than as an error, which is worse. A category is offered only where that student has `MINIMUM` problems within `REACH`, the same stance as `Curriculum::MINIMUM_PER_TOPIC`. Automatic matching respects both sides: `compatible?` keeps a student out of a room about something they did not ask for, and `agreed_categories` narrows a pairing to the overlap, so neither player gets a match on a topic only the other one wanted; a room picked by hand needs neither, because they read the categories and chose. The problems come from `Dispatcher.pick_shared`, which aims at the midpoint of the two ratings — or at the weaker player when they are further apart than `ChallengeMatchmaker::MAX_GAP`, which only an invitation or a hand-picked room reaches — takes the room's categories, drops topics either player has deferred or has not reached at school (`held_topic_ids`, unless the room named the topic), and takes one problem per template. `ChallengeScoring` pays `CORRECT_POINTS` for a correct answer plus up to `SPEED_POINTS` scaled by how much of the per-problem window was left, so a fast wrong answer is worth nothing and two correct answers always beat one. Durations are measured server-side from `challenge_participants.question_started_at` (stamped when a problem is served, cleared on answer), so the speed bonus can be neither forged by the client nor reset by reloading. `ChallengeSubmission` grades through `Grading`, pays XP per answer and records the daily streak, then finalizes when both players are done or the clock runs out — points decide it, a points tie goes to the lower total time, a tie on both (or a match nobody scored in) is a draw — awarding `Xp::CHALLENGE_WIN_BONUS`/`CHALLENGE_DRAW_BONUS`/`CHALLENGE_PLAY_BONUS` and the `duel_win`/`duel_wins_10` badges. A duel deliberately touches **no** measurement: no per-topic skill update, no question Elo, no `user_answers` row, so calibration and the dispatcher never read answers given under race conditions as evidence about a student. `ChallengeRecord` is a pure query over finished matches. The screens are driven by `challenge_controller.js` polling `GET /challenges/:id/state` (the app has no ActionCable setup); the server is the only authority on the clock, the scores and when a match ends, and opponents are shown by nickname only, like the leaderboard. The rooms tab opens on a white card whose one primary is „Намери противник", with the categories as chips in the open; invitations, open rooms, friends and history are `.duel-list`/`.duel-row` lists (a friend's row carries a „Още" menu holding „Премахни", on the account menu's `menu` controller), the record is one line over the history, and history rows reopen the result. The scoreboard's `.duel-side` folds to name-and-score over full-width bars on a phone; the match uses the practice screen's `.practice-layout.is-split` on a landscape tablet, and `duel.spec.mjs` checks at 1024×700 that the clock, the speed meter and every answer are on screen without scrolling. „Реванш" (`POST /challenges/:id/rematch`, `ChallengeMatchmaker.rematch!`) is offered against anyone for `Challenge::REMATCH_WINDOW` after the match — the same format and categories, as an invitation to the person just played — and after that only against a friend (to `/friends/:id/duel`), otherwise „Нов двубой". Two players who want each other are one room: `invite!` locks the pair's rows and the second press (a rematch or a friend invitation either way round) takes the seat in the first one's invitation (`invite_between`) — two children pressing „Реванш" side by side used to each wait in a room of their own. The result screen's buttons are a polled turbo-frame (`challenges/_rematch`, `GET /challenges/:id/rematch`), so the other player sees „… иска реванш!" and „Приемам реванша" without reloading, and the invitations on `/challenges` refresh the same way (`GET /challenges/invites`). „Класация" shows the top ten (`?all=1` for everyone) and each league's top five in place. The duel surfaces carry the **v2 design** (`Duels.dc.html` in the Claude Design project): the scoreboard has its own band instead of borrowing the practice header — each player is a block, the shared clock is a `conic-gradient` ring whose fill *is* the time left (red under ten seconds), and one bar per problem replaces the dots. Under the stem sits the **speed meter**, which says „по-бързо = повече точки" rather than explaining it: the bar drains with the window the bonus is scored against and the number beside it is what answering this instant would pay. It is display only — `ChallengeScoring` still works the real figure out server-side from `question_started_at` — and it is drawn from the server's own `elapsed`, re-anchored on every poll, so a reload cannot make it read full again. The result screen compares the two players problem by problem with the seconds that earned the difference and marks the fastest correct answer in the match (computed in the view: it is a property of those five rows and of nothing else); a win gets confetti, seeded so reopening the screen from the history shows the same fall, and hidden under `prefers-reduced-motion`. These screens are also the first users of the v2 **ink** ramp (`--color-ink-*`, Tailwind's gray nudged toward the brand hue) and the tactile `--edge-*` shadows; the rest of the app is still on `gray-*` and moves across a screen at a time rather than in one sweep nobody can review. The v2 primitives are demoed at `/design-system` under „Двубой". Two things in the design were deliberately not taken: its searching copy says the match starts the instant somebody joins, which the ready room replaced, and its nav uses lucide icons from unpkg, which this app does not do — every asset is vendored. Duels have no hints and no "не съм го учил" — both would be worth points to whoever used them fastest. An invitation is an ordinary room with `challenges.invited_user_id` set, which is the whole feature: it reuses the lobby, the ready room, the scoring and `pair` unchanged, and the id does three things — it keeps the room out of `open_lobbies` (so no stranger can see or join it), out of `candidates` (so matchmaking cannot eat it), and gives it `INVITE_TTL` rather than `LOBBY_TTL`, because a room waiting on a friend noticing is a different length of wait from one waiting on a queue. `ChallengesController#rematched` refuses to re-match an invited room for the same reason — its owner is waiting on one named person, and pairing them with the first stranger in the queue would leave the friend accepting an invitation to an abandoned room. The waiting screen names the friend instead of saying „Търсим противник…", which for an invitation is simply not true. A friend room is also the one duel whose **format** is chosen rather than given: `/friends/:id/duel` picks the number of problems (`Challenge::QUESTION_COUNTS`), the seconds each (`SECONDS_PER_QUESTION_OPTIONS`) and the categories, and `POST /friends/:id/invite` opens it. The public queue deliberately has none of this — it pairs strangers, and a format one of them chose is a format the other never agreed to. A screen of its own rather than controls on every row: the friends list is read at a glance to find who is here, and a form per row cannot be nested inside the row's own „Премахни" form anyway. Two layers guard the numbers, doing different jobs — `Challenge.permitted_format` falls back to the house format for anything off the menu (a stale or fiddled form is not worth an error page, the same stance as `DuelCategories.selected`), while the column validations only refuse the absurd, because a spec building a one-question room is legitimate and a closed list on the column would refuse it to stop a five-hundred-question one. **Difficulty is deliberately not offered**: the problems sit at the midpoint of the two ratings, which is what makes a duel between friends at different levels fair, and letting the inviter set it would mostly be a way to pick on somebody weaker — `duel_setup.fairness` says so on the screen rather than leaving its absence to be noticed.

**An invitation rings the friend's devices** (push notifications, the one kind the app sends). `ChallengeMatchmaker.invite!` enqueues `DuelInviteNotificationJob` only when it *opened* a new invitation — pressing again on one you sent, or accepting by inviting back, rings nobody — and the job sends nothing once the invitation is no longer in `Challenge.open_invites`. `WebPushDelivery` sends to every `push_subscriptions` row of the invited child („Покана за двубой", „X те кани на двубой…" by nickname, opening `/challenges`), tagged per invitation and with a TTL no longer than what is left of `INVITE_TTL`; a 404/410 from the push service deletes the row, the only way to learn a device is gone. It is switched on by `VAPID_PUBLIC_KEY`/`VAPID_PRIVATE_KEY` in the environment, like `Mailing` — without them no switch is shown and nothing is sent. The opt-in is „Покани за двубой" (`shared/_push_switch`, `push_controller.js`): a row in Настройки under Тренировка, and an offer at the top of the duel's side rail that disappears once taken. Rows are per **`current_user`** × endpoint, because a household tablet is passed between children: the browser holds one subscription, the server which children on it want it, and turning it off deletes only this child's row. The permission prompt is asked inside the tap (browsers refuse it otherwise); on an iPhone or iPad push works only from the home screen, so there the switch is shown disabled with a line pointing at „Аз"'s install row; a browser-blocked permission says so. Playwright's Chromium cannot subscribe (no push-service keys), so the browser half was checked with a faked `PushManager` and a push delivered through DevTools; the server half is `spec/services/duel_invite_push_spec.rb`.
